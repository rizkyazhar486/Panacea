Files
-----
  <organ>.glb                  3D model, one per organ
  <organ>/organ.webp           the organ itself
  <organ>/location.webp        where it sits in the body
  <organ>/microscopic.webp     tissue appearance
  <organ>/compare.webp         size comparison
  <organ>/thumb.webp           180x180 thumbnail. Added in the same import commit (42a3ed3ad) as the files
                               above; the upstream repository was not re-inspected when this line was
                               written, so its derivation from organ.webp is recorded, not verified.

Organs: brain, eyeball, heart, intestine, kidneys, liver, lungs,
        pancreas, skin

What these models ARE, and are NOT
----------------------------------
These models are AI-GENERATED. Each .glb contains a single mesh named
"tripo_node_<uuid>", produced by the Tripo 3D generator. They are NOT
derived from scans, dissection, or any measured human anatomy.

Two consequences, both stated on screen rather than hidden:

  1. The shapes are APPROXIMATIONS that resemble the organ. They are good
     for recognising general form and position. They are not a reference
     for anything that depends on exact morphology — vessel branching,
     chamber proportions, calyceal number, and the like.

  2. Each file is one undivided mesh with no named substructures, so the
     surface cannot be tapped to identify a part the way the full-body
     figure can. Parts are marked instead with manually placed coordinate
     hotspots, each carrying its Terminologia Anatomica term as a
     language-independent identity.

The full-body figure elsewhere in this application is NOT from this
source. It uses Z-Anatomy / BodyParts3D (CC BY-SA 4.0), which is derived
from real human data — see public/anatomy/CREDITS.txt. The two are kept
separate deliberately, and the difference is declared to the reader.
