Body Exposure canonical bodies (web derivatives, LOD2/LOD3)
===========================================================

adult_male.*  — derived from Z-Anatomy (BodyParts3D, DBCLS), CC BY-SA 4.0.
               External anal sphincter: BodyParts3D, (c) The Database Center for Life Science,
               CC Attribution-Share Alike 2.1 Japan (now also offered under CC BY 4.0).
                Includes Panacea-reconstructed intervertebral discs and pericardium
                (derivatives, same licence). See bodyexposure/PROVENANCE.md.
adult_female.* — derived from HuBMAP CCF 3D Reference Object Library, VH_Female
                (NLM Visible Human female), CC BY 4.0.
module_tissue_lymph_node.* — HuBMAP CCF library lymph node tissue model, CC BY 4.0.

Educational models. Not patient-specific anatomy and not a clinical tool.

pediatric_*  — derived from ICRP Publication 156, Paediatric Mesh-type Reference
               Computational Phantoms (2024), ICRP. Newborn, 1, 5, 10 and 15 years,
               male and female; CT-based reference phantoms. Dosimetry-only layers
               removed; laterality corrected where the source label contradicted the
               geometry (15-year female ovaries). Redistribution in Panacea cleared
               by the project owner (2026-10-03).

icrp_adult_* — derived from ICRP Publication 145, Adult Mesh-type Reference
               Computational Phantoms (2020), ICRP. Reference adult male (176 cm)
               and female (163 cm); CT-based phantoms adjusted to ICRP Publication 89
               reference values. Dosimetry-only layers removed. Redistribution in
               Panacea cleared by the project owner (2026-10-03, ICRP-derived meshes).

vhf_denver_ct_adult_female.* — Visible Human Female lower-extremity musculoskeletal geometry
               (pelvis to feet), University of Denver Center for Orthopaedic Biomechanics,
               CC BY 4.0, doi:10.56902/COB.vh.2022.0. Cite: Andreassen TE, Hume DR, Hamilton LD,
               Walker KE, Higinbotham SE, Shelburne KB. Three Dimensional Lower Extremity
               Musculoskeletal Geometry of the Visible Human Female and Male. Scientific Data
               10:34 (2023), doi:10.1038/s41597-022-01905-2. Segmented from the NLM Visible Human
               Female cryosections: Courtesy of the U.S. National Library of Medicine. Not endorsed
               by NLM or the University of Denver. Rotated, centred and decimated for the web;
               this data may not reflect NLM's most current Visible Human data.
               Skull, spine, ribs, sternum, clavicles, scapulae and humeri: machine segmentation of the
               Visible Human Female CT with TotalSegmentator (task "total", Apache-2.0; Wasserthal J et al.,
               Radiology: Artificial Intelligence 2023, doi:10.1148/ryai.230024), smoothed and decimated;
               checked against the Denver segmentation for femur, hip bone and sacrum only.
               One individual (donor 59 y, 157 cm, 88 kg), not an atlas. Not clinically reviewed.

fetus.*      — SVRTK fetal MRI atlas repository, King's College London, Centre for the Developing Brain
               (https://gin.g-node.org/kcl_cdb/fetal_body_mri_atlas), CC0 1.0. Uus A, Hall M, Grigorescu I, et al.
               Automated body organ segmentation, volumetry and population-averaged atlas for 3D motion-corrected
               T2-weighted fetal body MRI. Scientific Reports 14:6637 (2024), doi:10.1038/s41598-024-57087-x.
               Population average of 17 normal fetuses (25 to 28 weeks per the preprint), organs only, not an individual.
               Marching-cubes surfaces, smoothed and decimated for the web. Not clinically reviewed.

adult_male.rig_rom.glb (Walk, Run clips) — motion from the CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture
               Database (subjects 07 and 09), retargeted and clamped to AAOS limits.
               The data used in this project was obtained from mocap.cs.cmu.edu.
               The database was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217.
