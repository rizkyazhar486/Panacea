# PANACEA UNIVERSAL HUMAN ACCEPTANCE STANDARD

## Status

**Normative universality and inclusion target beneath the Panacea Constitution, One OS doctrine and System 10/10 Standard.**

The owner's aspiration is maximal human universality: Panacea should remain useful, dignified, trustworthy and understandable for any human being regardless of location, language, literacy, ability, age, socioeconomic context, connectivity, care setting or jurisdiction.

This is a design and validation target, not a claim that every individual human will prefer the same interface or that current evidence covers every population on Earth. "Across the universe" is interpreted as a maximal universality aspiration for human use wherever humans may live; no extraterrestrial or off-world deployment claim is implied without evidence.

## 1. Universal does not mean identical

A single rigid interface is not universal.

Panacea uses:

**one canonical human/patient state + one orchestration architecture + adaptive presentation and boundary adapters**

The core truth should remain coherent while language, interaction mode, clinical pathway, accessibility support, payer/jurisdiction rules, device availability and local workflow may differ.

## 2. Human acceptance is a weakest-link property

Let each verified universality dimension be scored in `[0,10]`:

- `A11Y` = accessibility and assistive-technology compatibility;
- `LANG` = language and localization adaptability;
- `LIT` = health/digital literacy adaptability;
- `CULT` = cultural and worldview respect without imposing one ideology;
- `AGE` = life-stage suitability where clinically supported;
- `ABILITY` = usability across sensory, motor, cognitive and communication differences;
- `ACCESS` = affordability and low-resource viability;
- `NET` = low-bandwidth/offline resilience;
- `DEVICE` = device and hardware independence where feasible;
- `ROLE` = role-aware usability;
- `JURIS` = jurisdiction and regulatory adaptability;
- `AGENCY` = consent, autonomy, data control and non-coercion;
- `DIGNITY` = respectful, non-stigmatizing and non-exploitative experience.

**UHA = min(A11Y, LANG, LIT, CULT, AGE, ABILITY, ACCESS, NET, DEVICE, ROLE, JURIS, AGENCY, DIGNITY)**

No overall "universal 10/10" claim is allowed while a critical subgroup or dimension remains untested or materially disadvantaged.

## 3. Permanent acceptance principles

Panacea should:
- preserve the same canonical patient identity/state across adaptations;
- support plain-language and expert views over the same truth;
- support localization of language, date/time, units and terminology without changing clinical meaning;
- support assistive technologies and accessible interaction patterns;
- keep critical actions understandable without requiring advanced technical literacy;
- work under constrained connectivity and lower-end hardware where the use case requires it;
- make uncertainty and unknowns understandable rather than hiding them;
- avoid dark patterns, coercive consent, manipulative urgency and engagement addiction;
- avoid unnecessary exclusion based on geography, income, disability, language or device ecosystem;
- adapt to local law, payer, terminology and workflow through boundary adapters rather than forking patient truth;
- preserve patient and clinician agency.

## 4. Standards-aware implementation targets

Where applicable, implementation should align with:
- W3C WCAG 2.2 AA or stronger accessibility targets for web interfaces;
- Unicode/CLDR-compatible localization patterns;
- standards-based healthcare interoperability such as HL7 FHIR and relevant terminology systems;
- locale-aware units, dates and number formatting;
- keyboard, screen-reader, focus, contrast and scalable-text support;
- responsive layouts that preserve critical actions on narrow/mobile screens;
- offline/store-and-forward workflows for intermittent connectivity.

Referencing a standard is not proof of compliance; conformance must be tested.

## 5. Universality test matrix

Validation should sample materially different contexts rather than only the easiest user: local and international languages; low, average and expert health literacy; keyboard-only, screen-reader, low-vision, motor-constraint and cognitive-load-sensitive flows; modern hospital, small clinic/Puskesmas, rural/remote and intermittent-connectivity settings; patient, caregiver, clinician, nurse/allied health, operations/admin and payer roles; and at least two materially different jurisdictions before claiming global adaptability.

## 6. Acceptance metrics

**Task success rate = successful predefined tasks / attempted tasks**

**Comprehension = correct understanding responses / scored responses**

**Access completion rate = users able to complete the critical workflow / eligible users tested**

**Adaptation parity gap = max subgroup performance - min subgroup performance**

A universal design claim must report subgroup results, not only the aggregate mean.

## 7. Compactness remains mandatory

Universality must not create menu explosion.

**Maximum orchestration depth, minimum visible complexity.**

Adaptation should happen through defaults, progressive disclosure, role-aware views, accessibility settings, localization, context-sensitive workflow and boundary adapters.

## 8. Human disagreement is allowed

"Acceptable to every kind of human being" does not mean forcing unanimous preference. The target is no unnecessary exclusion, no coercion, no hidden discrimination, no preventable accessibility barrier, no avoidable cultural disrespect, transparent trade-offs and user choice where clinically and operationally possible.

## 9. Evidence rule

Each universality dimension progresses through:

`target → implemented → technically verified → representative-user evaluated → externally validated`

Unknown remains unknown.

The machine-readable acceptance matrix lives in `governance/UNIVERSAL_HUMAN_ACCEPTANCE.json`.

## 10. North-star expression

**Any human. Any care setting. One trusted longitudinal care flow — adapted locally, governed consistently, and kept simple.**
